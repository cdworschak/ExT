Exploration

What happens between fights — reading a room, and covering ground.

Travel

GM rolls a d6 for days until a Travel Event (d8 with a relevant wilderness talent or toolkit; −1 on treacherous terrain).

The d8 upgrade isn't the only lever. A relevant Skill, Contingency, Talent, or other resource a player can plausibly justify may also improve travel odds or let the party bypass a rolled event entirely — GM's discretion, same judgment call used everywhere else in this system.

d6Event
1Mishap — add 1 day
2–3Random Encounter
4–5Failed to Gather Food — mark a ration or become Deprived
6Good Fortune — subtract 1 day
Worked Example — A Travel Roll

The party needs 5 days to reach their destination on foot. The GM rolls a d6 for how many days pass before the first Travel Event: a 3. Since 3 doesn't exceed the 5-day journey, an event happens — 3 days in, with 2 days remaining.

A separate roll determines which event: the GM rolls the Travel Event table itself, a fresh d6 — a 2 comes up: Random Encounter, resolved using the region's encounter table.

That resolved, the whole process repeats against the new remaining length (2 days). The GM rolls the timing d6 again: a 4. Since 4 exceeds the 2 days actually left, the party arrives without any further event — the roll doesn't get looked up on the Travel Event table at all, because it never needed to be.

Two different d6 rolls doing two different jobs: one asks when, compared against how much journey is left; the other, rolled separately and only when the first roll says an event happens at all, asks what.

Encounter Tables

Every "roll on the region's encounter table" reference in this document assumes one exists — build it before the party travels there, not during. A good regional encounter table is 8 entries (roll a d8), mixing genuine danger with texture that isn't combat at all.

  • 4–5 entries of real danger — something that can genuinely hurt the party if handled badly. Build these with Enemy Design Philosophy if nothing existing fits.
  • 2–3 entries of texture, not combat — a sign of danger already passed, a landmark, a fellow traveler, weather worth describing.
  • 1 entry that's genuinely good news — a shortcut, a safe camp, something useful found.
  • Reuse Reaction Check for anything with a mind of its own rather than baking disposition into the table itself.

Exploration Procedure

A player's own specific, declared action always comes first — "I check under the rug," "I run my hand along the statue's base." These get resolved on their own terms, GM's call on what check applies. The four actions below are only the default behaviors for a Turn when nothing more specific is being declared.

Each Turn (10 minutes) while exploring a site, the party takes one of the following, then the GM rolls the Exploration Event check.

  • Move — travel to an adjacent area or room. No check needed.
  • Search — a careful, deliberate look for hidden doors, traps, or overlooked treasure. One roll for the whole party — 2d10 + the highest INT bonus among the searching characters — vs the location's Target (12 by default, shiftable per Scenario Target Modifiers). Ties up the whole Turn.
  • Listen at a door — a quick check before committing to a room, doesn't use up the Turn's main action. Roll 2d10+WIS vs the location's Target.
  • Rest — recover Guard or Grit. Still rolls the Exploration Event check like any other Turn.

1Encounter — roll on the location's encounter table
2Encounter Sign — a 1–2 on the next roll becomes that encounter
