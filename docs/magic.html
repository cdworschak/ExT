02 — Magic

Magic

Possession-based casting, the Overcast Backlash Pool, spell resolution, and procedural spell names — split out from Foundations, which had quietly become 70% magic content by volume.

Magic Baseline

Possession, not consumption. Casting means possessing enough shards to pay a tier's cost, not consuming a fixed daily resource. Cantrip tier requires only possessing ≥1 shard as a focus, never spent — real mechanical effects (Spark and above) require actual casting access, not just carrying shards.

Shards are two separate economic goods, not one. Raw shards (dust) — loose, unrefined, 5cp each — power Cantrip and Spark. Refined shards — cut and processed, 10gp each — power Minor and above. A Wandslinger's entire build only ever touches raw dust; they never pay refined-shard prices, since they're hard-capped below Minor.

Overcasting

Only applies to Minor, Moderate, Major, and Mythic — the refined-shard economy. There is no overcasting Cantrip or Spark. Those two run on raw shards (dust) with a flat, known cost — nothing to fall short of, since the cost is never uncertain. A character who can't afford Spark's flat cost simply can't cast it.

A caster can always attempt a cast even without enough shards to pay for it — but the spell only actually manifests on a genuinely good roll. Resolved entirely with a d6 pool, nothing to calculate.

  1. Pool size by tier: Minor 4d6, Moderate 3d6, Major 2d6, Mythic 1d6.
  2. More than half the cost missing? Remove one die. No floor — the pool can hit 0.
  3. Pool at 0: instant death, no roll. The only way to fail this hard — only reachable attempting Mythic tier with less than half the cost on hand.
  4. Add rerolls equal to CON bonus — reroll that many lowest dice once each, keep the better result. CON never adds dice, only rerolls existing ones, so pool size stays strictly tied to tier.
  5. Roll the pool, read the highest die.
Highest dieResult

Success is gated to a natural 6 specifically so a reckless, high-CON attempt at Mythic tier with nothing invested can't succeed through sheer volume of tries — tested against 500 independent "keep attempting until success or death" simulations, and none ever succeeded. The first reckless attempt is always fatal.

Spellcasting Procedure

Spell Resolution: Attack vs. Effect

  • Attack spells — aimed at a target, competes with a weapon's role. Caster rolls to-hit like a weapon, plus shards cost.
  • Effect spells — everything else (wards, buffs, heals, utility). No caster roll. Willing target: works automatically. Unwilling target: target rolls a resistance check.

Spell Names

Form and Adjective are shared across all traditions (64 entries each, rollable as a real d8.d8 table). Noun is split by tradition — Arcane (Shardbound), Holy (Flamesworn), Nature (Wildspoken), also 64 entries each.

Spell Name Acquisition

  1. The Ritual — consume 50 shards, a full day, GM rolls a fully random name.
  2. Name-Crafting (level 3+) — combine parts of known names into a new one, player-directed.
  3. Diegetic Discovery — learn a name through play. No shard cost, tied to a story beat.

Starting spell name and any free name from Advancement can be chosen instead of rolled once Name-Crafting is unlocked (level 3+).
