07 — Inventory & Items

Inventory & Items

Item slots, weapons, armor, mundane gear, and magic items — everything a character can carry, in one place.

Item Slots

Ten item slots total: 2 hand, 2 body, 6 backpack. Hand slots hold what's actively wielded; body slots hold worn gear for easy access in combat; backpack slots hold everything else.

Using something already in hand costs your normal action for the turn — attacking, drinking a held potion, activating a held gadget, all the same, no different from anything else you might do with your action. Producing or stowing an item is also a full action, not an add-on — drawing a new weapon, swapping what's in a hand slot, retrieving something from a body slot, or from a backpack slot (Many Pockets removes the cost for backpack retrieval specifically). Since a turn only has one action, producing something and using it both happen in the same turn only if it was already in hand; otherwise it's draw this turn, use it next. This applies to shards and dust too — a caster needs a hand holding them to cast; see Magic Baseline.

  • Shards and coin are abstracted resources, not slot items — tracked as numbers, never occupying a slot regardless of quantity.
  • A slot holds one distinct item, or a reasonable stack of a single consumable type (a quiver of arrows, several days of rations, a handful of torches).
  • At capacity, picking up something new means dropping or stowing something else first.
  • Shield/Tower Shield sit in a hand slot, same category as a weapon.

Every named weapon shares its tier's exact die, price, and rules — the name is flavor and a hook for keywords, not a separate mechanical identity. The Diegetic Note column has no mechanical effect; it's how the weapon reads to other people in the world.

Heavy weapons are always Two-handed. All ranged weapons are Two-handed too, at any tier — reloading needs both hands regardless of size. Only Light/Medium melee can pair with a Shield.

Melee vs. ranged is a separate axis from which stat governs the weapon. A DEX-based finesse weapon (rapier, dagger) is still melee. Finesse applies to light, bladed weapons designed for precision and speed over raw force — not blunt weapons (clubs, maces, hammers) and not polearms (spears, staves, pikes), regardless of tier. A weapon needs to be individually marked Finesse to use DEX; Light or Medium tier alone doesn't qualify it. Only genuinely ranged weapons are always Two-handed, regardless of which stat governs them.

Casting stat and weapon stat are always separate. A caster's cast stat is never used for weapon damage, even if it's their defining stat.

Ranged weapons of every type are capped at the same zone or one adjacent zone, full stop — no weapon reaches further than that.

Toolkits & Usage

Every item — weapons, armor, toolkits, torches, rations — has three usage dots. When all three are filled, it's depleted or destroyed. What fills a dot differs by item type, but the shared shape means only one rule to learn:

Item typeUsage dot trigger
Weapons/Armor/AmmoRoll a d6 after combat for each item used; on a 1–3, mark a dot
Torches/Light sourcesMark a dot whenever a relevant exploration roll calls for it
RationsMark a dot when taking a long rest without first taking the time to hunt or forage
ToolkitsMark a dot every time it's actually used for its purpose
Everything elseGM's call

Weapons and armor can be fully repaired for a quarter of their original price. Depleted toolkits and consumables need outright replacing. Selling: items generally sell for half their listed price, better with a CHA-based check or real rapport with the buyer.

Worked Example — An Item Depletes

Rivet's Heavy weapon has three empty usage dots. After a fight where he used it, the GM rolls a d6 — a 2 comes up, 1–3 marks a dot. One dot filled.

Two fights later, the same roll happens twice more: a 1, then a 3 — both mark a dot. The weapon now has all three dots filled — it's depleted, broken or too worn to use until repaired.

Rivet gets it fixed for a quarter of its original price (a Heavy weapon costs 60gp, so repair costs 15gp) — the dots clear, and it's back to full condition. If it had been a Healing Toolkit or a set of torches instead of a weapon, there'd be no repair option at all: consumables that deplete need replacing outright, not fixing.

Toolkits are bundles of similarly-themed items that share one item slot.

Magic Items

Charged/consumables priced at the tier's casting cost. Permanent items priced at 10–20× the tier's average casting cost — Cantrip/Spark in raw shards, Minor and above in refined shards.

Magic Items, Eberron-Original

Every item below uses only the same four levers everything else in this document uses — Advantage/Disadvantage, a Target shift, a new capability, or a resource effect — never a flat roll bonus. No "+1 sword" style items; that's a d20 holdover this system's own design constraint rules out. Magic item creation also requires a real workspace and resources, GM's discretion — see Archetypes → Artificer.
