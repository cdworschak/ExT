04 — Archetypes

Archetypes

Three chassis: Wayfarer (martial), Agent (skills/utility), Syberist (spellcaster, four traditions including Artificer). Full kits, resources, and every option each one can take.

Universal — all four archetypes Every archetype also grows on a slower Vertical track regardless of chassis: at levels 2, 4, 6, and 8, +1 to one stat (player's choice) and +1 flat Guard. Total by level 8: +4 stat, +4 flat Guard. The tables below cover the Lateral track — the archetype-specific options layered on top, every level.

Wayfarer

Fighter/Barbarian chassis — Grit resource, a Guard bonus of +2, and a full martial toolkit through Grit Maneuvers. Grit starts at 2, +1 at even levels. +1 Grit per Turn of rest, if not Deprived, up to your max. A full pool (6 at most, by level 8) is reachable within an hour of rest — unlike Wounds, which Turns of rest don't touch at all.

Grit Maneuvers

All maneuvers are available from level 1 — gated by Grit cost, not character level. The stronger ones just cost more Grit.

One Maneuver per action, no stacking. Each Maneuver either is your action for the turn (Guard Stance, Taunt, Rally, Read the Fight) or modifies your one attack (Focus, Cleave, Guarded Strike). Since a turn only grants one attack, only one attack-modifying Maneuver can ever apply to a single attack — Focus and Cleave can't both land on the same swing. Maneuvers combine across different PCs' turns, or across different rounds for the same PC, never by stacking two modifiers onto one action.

Worked Example — Spending Grit

Rivet (a Warbred Wayfarer, 4 Grit) shares a zone with Ilyara, a fragile Shardbound Syberist. Three bandits are about to act, and Rivet knows they'll all target her. On his turn, he spends 2 Grit on Shield: until his next turn, every attack against Ilyara is rolled at Disadvantage.

All three bandits attack Ilyara this Round. Standard-tier mooks with no attack exception roll a flat 2d10 vs Target 12 — 45% to hit normally. At Disadvantage, that drops to 22%. Two of the three miss; only one connects, and for a fraction of the damage a clean hit would have dealt.

Rivet has 2 Grit left. Next Round, he could spend 1 on Guard Stance to protect himself instead, or save it — Grit refills at +1 per Turn of rest, so nothing carries over stress-free mid-fight; every spend is a real trade-off between protecting now and having options later.

Dual-wield

One attack roll, roll both weapons' damage dice, take the higher. Main-hand Light or Medium, off-hand must be Light, both sharing the same governing stat. Applies identically to unarmed strikes (main fist Medium via Iron Palm, off-hand fist Light).

Trophies (level 3)

After defeating a creature in combat, take a trophy (1 item slot). Agent

Rogue/Ranger/Bard/Diplomat chassis — Leverage resource, Skills, and Contingency declarations. Width instead of raw power.

Leverage & Contingency

Leverage starts at 2, +1 per level. Regenerates during downtime, once per lull in the action — not from resting a Turn, and deliberately not locked to any one setting. The player describes how they're using the downtime to prepare; the GM decides which mental stat (CHA/WIS/INT) actually fits that description, then rolls 2d10 + that stat vs Target 12. Success regenerates 1 Leverage. The GM may shift the Target based on how favorable the environment is — a barren cave with WIS chosen might push the Target up; Sharn with CHA chosen might pull it down. Can be spent on an ally's behalf, not just your own declarations.

Contingency is what Leverage buys: retroactively "already having prepared" for a specific moment. Cost scales with the size of the ask, GM-adjudicated the same way potency tiers are priced.

  • 1 Leverage — a small, plausible flashback ("I have rope," "I've been to this district before")
  • 2 Leverage — a medium one ("I've dealt with this NPC before," "I bribed a guard on the way in")
  • 3+ Leverage — something that reshapes the scene ("I already turned this guard captain")
Worked Example — Declaring a Contingency

Vex, a Rogue-Agent, is being chased across Sharn's rooftops and hits a gap between buildings too wide to jump. She has no rope in her inventory — nothing on her sheet says she planned for this.

She spends 1 Leverage and declares: "I looped a line across this gap on the way up." No roll — Contingency is never rolled, at any cost. The GM prices the proposal by size, the player pays it, and it's simply true from that point on.

Vex is down to 1 Leverage until her next chance to regenerate it — a real Turn of downtime spent casing, networking, or otherwise preparing, with a 2d10+stat check deciding whether that pays off. The regeneration check rolls; the Contingency spend itself never does.

Skills

Choose freely at creation and on level-up: Swift (no Disengage check needed, Advantage on movement-related checks), Many Pockets (+1 backpack slot per pick — repeatable, same shape as Expertise below).

Network — a real, named contact tied to a specific domain (a fence, a guard captain, a dockworkers' foreman). Using that relationship for the Leverage-regeneration check above grants Advantage on the roll — naturally CHA-flavored, most available wherever that contact could plausibly be found, but never the only path to Leverage. A Contingency drawing on your contact's domain also costs 1 less Leverage (minimum 1).

Expertise — choose a narrow field of specific expertise, same granularity as a Background Talent ("forgery and false documents," "appraising valuables," "reading a crowd's mood"). Gain Advantage on checks within that field, at will. Can be taken multiple times, each pick adding a new distinct field — player-invented, not drawn from the Background Talent pool. No separate cap needed: taking it repeatedly means forgoing the other Skills, the same opportunity cost every other choice here carries.

Agent Combat Techniques

A real menu, chosen in the moment like a Grit Maneuver — not a passive pick-once Skill. Resourced entirely by action economy and trigger rarity, no new currency.

TechniqueEffect
Precision StrikeThis attack has Disadvantage, but deals +2 flat damage if it hits
Exploit the OpeningOnce per Wound that opens on a target (yours or an ally's), your next hit against them with a Light or Medium weapon automatically counts as a critical
Take AimSpend your action aiming (no attack this turn); your next attack this fight against that target, with a Light or Medium weapon, gets Advantage, and rolls a second weapon damage die on top of the normal hit — that second die's result also permanently reduces their Guard, surviving even a Wound refill
Worked Example — Take Aim

Nix (a Gnome Agent, DEX +2, Medium crossbow) is up against Kolt, a Standard-tier Captain. Round 1: Nix spends his whole action aiming instead of attacking — no damage this Round, but Kolt is now marked.

Round 2: Nix finally lands the shot. The attack rolls at Advantage (3d10, keep the best two), and on a hit rolls a second Medium weapon die (1d6) on top of the normal damage. Say the bonus die comes up 4 — Kolt takes the normal hit as usual, and his max Guard drops by 4, permanently, for the rest of the fight. Even if Kolt takes a Wound later and refills to his new max, that max is now 4 lower than it would have been.

The cost was real: a full Round where Nix dealt zero damage, for one attack that hits harder and leaves a lasting mark. Against a target that dies fast anyway, that's a bad trade — Take Aim earns its keep against something built to survive a long fight.

Agent Flavors

Three concept archetypes on the same chassis, differing only in what kind of Contingency each leans into and what regenerates Leverage.

Rogue / "Cutthroat" — physical, logistical Contingencies. Leverage regenerates by casing a location before the job. Pairs with Exploit the Opening/Take Aim and Many Pockets.

Diplomat / "Emissary" — relational, informational Contingencies. Leverage regenerates through successful Network use and political downtime. Pairs with cross-Background Talent access.

Bard / "Raconteur" — social, reputational Contingencies. Leverage regenerates through performance checks. The team-facing Leverage spend is the whole point here — handing an ally a Contingency is the direct mechanical cousin of Bardic Inspiration.

Wandslinger

Agent and Wayfarer may trade into minor casting access — hard-capped at Cantrip/Spark, never Minor or above. Not available to Syberist, who already has inherent casting that exceeds it.

  • Agent: trades one Skill choice for Spark-tier access. Shard Bolt counts as an eligible attack for Exploit the Opening and Take Aim, treated as a Light weapon.
  • Wayfarer: permanently forgoes access to one Advanced Maneuver of their choice, in exchange for Spark-tier access.

Syberist

Wizard/Sorcerer/Cleric/Druid/Artificer chassis — real spellcasting, four traditions differing in cast stat, Spell Names flavor, and — for Artificer specifically — how casting actually resolves.

  • Shardbound (Wizard/Sorcerer) — cast stat INT. Arcane, raw dragonshard/planar power.
  • Flamesworn (Cleric/Paladin) — cast stat CHA. Silver Flame or House-oath channeling.
  • Wildspoken (Druid) — cast stat WIS. Eldeen Reaches / Gatekeeper tradition.
  • Artificer (Artificer) — cast stat INT. Build now, unleash later — see below, since this one works differently enough to need its own section.

Artificer, in Detail

Started as its own fourth archetype (Cannith-Touched), rebuilt into a Syberist tradition after three separate problems traced back to the same root cause: the whole Infusion subsystem was 5e's Artificer mechanic imported wholesale into a document otherwise built on OSR conventions. Every fix attempted was patching around that mismatch rather than fixing it. Matches Wildspoken's own precedent — a Druid didn't need to be its own archetype either.

Quick Rig — the tradition's baseline casting method, free from level 1. Cantrip and Spark cast normally, reactive, in combat, same as any Syberist. Minor tier and above never happens reactively in combat at all. Instead, during downtime, Name-Craft a spell exactly as any Syberist would, but seal the effect into a crafted item instead of firing it immediately. That item triggers later as a single action, releasing the effect — by the Artificer Syberist, or by whoever else is holding it.

Building a Contraption takes 1 Turn (10 minutes) of dedicated work — cannot be rushed, cannot happen mid-combat. Known capacity: 1 at level 1, +1 at level 4, +1 at level 8 (3 max) — how many built-but-unused Contraptions can exist at once. Triggering one uses it up.

The trigger roll always uses INT — but the holder's own INT, not the crafter's. Handing a Contraption to someone else is a genuine gamble, not a strictly-better play, since their own INT bonus governs whether it goes off.

Worked Example — Quick Rig

Cassa, a level-4 Artificer Syberist (INT +2, 2 known Contraption capacity), spends downtime before a raid Name-Crafting a Minor-tier attack spell into a Shock Coil (1d8+INT when triggered) instead of casting it immediately.

The plan splits the party: Cassa stays with the main group, while Rurik (a Heavy-weapon Wayfarer, INT −1) breaks off to cover a side entrance alone. She hands him the Shock Coil before they split up. If he ends up needing it, the trigger roll uses his INT, not hers — a real gamble, since her INT +2 would land it far more reliably than his INT −1.

That's the actual trade-off: Cassa could keep every Contraption on herself for the best odds, but then Rurik goes into a fight alone with nothing but his weapon. Accepting worse odds on the trigger roll is the price of having real coverage in two places at once — not a fallback for when something goes wrong, but a deliberate call made before it does.

Permanent items use the existing 10–20× pricing, with a real choice of where to land: roll the tier's own cost die for baseline crafting days, pay the top of the range to halve that time, pay the bottom to double it. Salvaging an existing magic item destroys it but recovers shards equal to the tier's base average casting cost — roughly 5–10% of what it was worth built, a real loss but useful for material nobody wants to carry.

Aberrant Mark — open to any Background, mark-touched by accident rather than House-blooded. Grants the ability to attempt a Minor-tier cast (Attack or Effect) with zero shards ever possessed — every attempt is a genuine Overcast, resolved entirely through the existing Overcast Backlash Pool (always reduced to 3d6, since 0 shards is always more than half short of any possible Minor cost). No new mechanic — reuses casting and Overcasting directly. Own Noun category for Spell Names: Aberrant.

Mark Tier Power Baseline

Least ≈ Spark tier (small, real, narrow), Lesser ≈ Minor tier (a real step up, reliable utility), Greater ≈ Major tier (rare, breaks the laws of nature), Siberys ≈ Mythic tier (unique, world-altering, essentially GM-placed only). Not an exact mechanical translation — a power-level anchor for what each tier should feel like when building one out.

Talents & Expertise

Talents grant Advantage on relevant checks. This is the universal expertise system every archetype has access to, tied to Background rather than class — every Background grants 2-3 named Talents already spanning every stat.

Agent's extra reach: at character creation, an Agent may take one additional Talent from any other Background's list.
