06 — Combat

Combat

Resolution, weapons, maneuvers, magic items, the economy, wounds, and everything that governs a fight from surprise to the last swing.

Combat Procedure

Core Resolution

Full mechanic and design philosophy on Foundations; stat generation and the Mercy rule live on Character Creation, since that's genuinely where rolling stats happens. Quick reference: roll 2d10 + stat bonus, succeed on 12 or higher. Advantage/Disadvantage: roll 3d10, keep the best or worst two. Critical Hit (doubles on the counted dice = max damage) and Damage Floor (minimum 1 damage on any hit) are also covered there — same rule for weapon attacks and Attack spells alike; Disadvantage never crits, no exceptions.

Economy

Raw shards: 5cp each (= 1 arrow), power Cantrip/Spark. Refined shards: 10gp each, power Minor+ and permanent magic items. Starting wealth: 200gp, flexible — spend on refined shards, gear, or both.

Status Effects

Four named, temporary states appear throughout this document. Stated once here, referenced by name everywhere else.

Guard & Wounds

Guard = 4 + Guard Stat bonus + archetype bonus + armor + shield, floor 1. Restores fully after a Turn of rest. 1 Wound slot at creation, second at level 4, third at level 8.

An open Wound reduces max Guard by 2; current Guard refills to the new max, floor 1. The next roll is made with Disadvantage ("Reeling"), plus a Wound Complication (d6). Wounds don't heal from resting a Turn and can't close mid-combat.

Wound Complication Table (d6)

Rolled once, when a Wound opens. One-time effects only — nothing compounds with a second Wound.

d6Complication
1Staggered Back — the blow throws you into an adjacent zone
2Grip Torn Loose — your weapon or dragonshard focus is knocked from your hand-slot; costs an action to retrieve
3Gear Scattered — a carried item (GM or player's choice, something non-essential) breaks, is lost, or spills its contents
4Reeling Hard — your Initiative order drops to last for the rest of the fight
5Off-Balance — your next zone move this fight requires a DEX-equivalent check or fails
6Rattled to the Bone — Disadvantage on your next two rolls instead of one

No stacking — see Status Effects above; a Wound opening while Reeling or Rattled is already pending doesn't add to it.

Worked Example — A Wound Opens

Rivet has 5 Guard left (max Guard 9, already worn down this fight) and takes a hit for 7 damage. Guard absorbs the first 5, hitting 0 — the remaining 2 damage overflows.

Since Rivet has an open Wound slot available, a Wound opens instead of him going down. Max Guard drops by 2, from 9 to 7. Current Guard doesn't stay at 0 — it refills to the new max: Rivet is now at 7/7 Guard, worse off going forward, but not staggering at zero.

Two more things happen the same moment: Rivet's next roll is made with Disadvantage ("Reeling"), and the GM rolls a d6 on the Wound Complication table — a 4 comes up: Reeling Hard, his Initiative drops to last for the rest of the fight.

If Rivet takes a second Wound later this fight, the same thing happens again on top of a smaller pool: max Guard drops another 2, from 7 to 5, refilling current Guard to 5. Wounds never heal from resting a Turn, and nothing closes them mid-combat — Field Dressing or real magic can close one Wound at a time out of combat, but a full day's rest closes all of Rivet's open Wounds at once, restoring his max Guard back to its full 9.

At 0 Guard with all Wound slots full: roll d6 — 1–3 dies, 4+ faints and rolls on the Scar Table below. A fainted character isn't safe yet: if their wounds aren't treated within a Turn by another character, they die anyway. Treating them requires a check using whichever stat fits the method (INT for a real medical attempt, WIS for a more instinctive one) — success stabilizes them for good.

Healing Magic

Combat Mending (in combat, costs an action + shards per tier): restores Guard, up to current max — Minor 1d8+stat, Moderate 2d8+stat, Major 2d8+stat, Mythic 4d8+stat, same dice as damage at each tier. Moderate tier and above also clears Reeling or Rattled from the target. Can't close a Wound under any circumstances. Field Dressing (out of combat, a full Exploration Turn): closes one Wound.

Zones & Range

Melee, Near, and Far are the GM's framework for adjudicating positioning within a real, shared scene. The scene comes first — the tavern, the rafters, the street outside — tracked collectively at the table like any theater-of-the-mind moment. What "Far" represents flexes with the scene: a hundred yards on an open field, or just "through the door" in a cramped tavern.

Each character reads Melee/Near/Far relative to themselves. Two people can both be Far from a melee cluster while being nowhere near each other. A character with a bow reads more of a room as Near than a melee-only character standing in the same spot, since their reach extends further. Real-world logic governs each reading; the framework keeps that logic consistent at the table.

Disengaging tracks the shared fiction. A melee-capable hostile close enough, in the group's shared imagination, to threaten you triggers a Disengage check on your way out — whether you just closed to attack them, or they moved in on you. This marks the line between an ongoing melee's give-and-take and the moment someone actually runs, which shifts the scene into a chase.

Multi-target and zone-based abilities (Cleave, Rally, "allies in your zone") use the acting character's own reading.

The GM has final say, using this framework to keep the call fast and consistent. Gaps get adjudicated at the table, like any theater-of-the-mind scene.

Worked Example — The Tavern

Rivet fights Bandit 1 at the bar. Nix watches from the rafters, bow drawn. Bandit 2 guards the door. Kessia just fled outside.

From Rivet: Bandit 1 is Melee. Nix and Bandit 2 are Near. Kessia is Far — she's left the building.
From Bandit 2: everyone is Near, Kessia included — she's standing right outside the door he's guarding.
From Nix: both bandits are Near, since her bow reaches them. If neither bandit carries a ranged weapon, they read Nix as Far — nothing in their hands reaches the rafters.

Same moment, three different readings, all correct at once.

Three zones: Melee, Near, Far. Move (0–1 zone) and Act are both available the same Round. Melee weapons hit same zone only; ranged/thrown hit same zone + 1 adjacent.

Disengaging: leaving a zone with a live melee hostile requires a DEX check (2d10+DEX vs 12) or they get a free attack first.

Worked Example — Rivet Retreats

Rivet (Guard 4/10, already carrying one Wound) is fighting two Blade-bandits in the Ditch and decides to fall back to the Road. Rolls the Disengage check: 4 vs 12, fails. The nearer bandit gets a free attack — rolled 14 vs 12, a hit, 2 damage to Guard. Rivet still reaches the Road; the failed check cost a parting shot, not the retreat itself.

Failing the check doesn't trap the character — it only costs a parting shot. The DEX check gates whether the melee hostile gets one free swing on the way out, not whether the retreat itself succeeds.

Ties: higher DEX bonus goes first; a quick reroll between tied parties only if that's also equal.

Every other source that touches Initiative, consolidated here rather than scattered: Reeling Hard (a Wound Complication result) overrides the roll entirely — Initiative order drops to last for the rest of the fight, not a numeric penalty stacked on the formula above. Not on my watch! (a Basic Grit Maneuver, 1 Grit) lets a Wayfarer reroll their own Initiative roll once, keeping the better result. Nothing else in this document currently touches Initiative.

Surprise & Ambushes

Each character rolls 2d10+WIS individually. First success can warn others (Advantage for anyone yet to roll). Failure means skipping the Round 1 action only.

Morale

Base Morale 7 (2d6 roll-under-or-equal to pass), +2 while a named leader is alive. Checks fire once each at: first casualty, 50% losses, leader down.
