Core Resolution

This system's actual design constraint, stated plainly: situational difficulty is represented by shifting the Target number or granting Advantage/Disadvantage, never by stacking small numeric modifiers. A GM has exactly two levers for "this is harder/easier than normal" — move the Target, or apply Advantage/Disadvantage. Every other number on a character sheet stays fixed; only the target and the dice pool ever move.

Check format: roll 2d10 + stat bonus, succeed on 12 or higher.

Advantage and Disadvantage never stack within a single roll, from any combination of sources. A given roll has Advantage, Disadvantage, or neither. If one specific roll would have both from different origins at once, they cancel — roll normally. This is strictly per-roll, not per-character — a PC's own outgoing attack and an enemy's incoming attack against that same PC are separate rolls that never interact, even in the same round. A PC with Advantage on their own next attack and an enemy attacking that PC with Disadvantage imposed on that roll are unrelated instances; nothing cancels, since nothing is happening on the same roll.

What's in This Document

A brief abstract and one concrete example of every other section — enough to orient yourself before diving into the full page for whichever part you actually need.

Character Creation

The full step-by-step build sequence, from choosing a Background through starting wealth, plus guidance on what makes each choice strong or weak. Example: your Guard Stat must differ from your weapon stat and cast stat — locked in at creation, never revisited. Full page →

Backgrounds

Race and culture fused into one character-creation choice — 45 options, each granting a stat bonus, two Talents, and narrative-only gear. Example: a Valenar Blademarked elf gets +1 DEX/STR, a war-spirit combat Talent, and a clan sigil — never a free weapon or mount with hidden stats. Full page →

Archetypes

Three chassis — Wayfarer (Grit, martial maneuvers), Agent (Leverage, Contingency, Skills), Syberist (real spellcasting, four traditions including Artificer). Example: a Wayfarer spends 2 Grit on Shield to impose Disadvantage on every attack against an ally this round. Full page →

Magic

Possession-based casting (you need enough shards on hand, not a daily spell-slot budget), six potency tiers from free Cantrips to world-altering Mythic effects, and the Overcast Backlash Pool for attempting a cast you can't quite afford. Example: a Minor-tier attack spell deals 1d6 + your cast stat, identical in shape to a Medium weapon — magic and steel compete on the same terms. Full page →

Combat

Guard-and-Wound HP model, zone-based positioning, weapon tiers, and the full Wayfarer Grit Maneuvers list. Example: a Wound reduces your max Guard by 2, and current Guard refills to that new, lower max — you're worse off going forward, not just hurt right now. Full page →

Exploration

Reaction checks for NPC disposition, and a d6-based random event roll for travel and dungeon exploration. Example: rolling a 1 on the Travel Event table means a Mishap — a full extra day added to the journey. Full page →

Enemy Design

Build any monster from a handful of numbers (Base Target, Guard, weapon tier, damage bonus) instead of a full stat block. Example: a Mook has no Wound slot at all — Guard alone decides when it's down. Full page →

Advancement

How PCs grow — XP via themed question packages, leveling, and the Vertical (universal stat/Guard growth) and Lateral (archetype-specific options) tracks. Example: level 2 needs a fresh 5 XP, level 3 a fresh 10 — the tally resets after each level, so no single lucky session ever skips a level. Full page →

Glossary

One canonical definition per term that's ever caused confusion or drift across this whole document. Example: Turn (a 10-minute exploration unit) and Round (one combat cycle) are genuinely different things that share no relationship — an earlier draft conflated them. Full page →

Languages

Common is free to everyone. INT bonus ≤ −2 means illiterate — speaks known languages but can't read or write any. INT bonus > 0 grants +1 extra language per point. Non-human Backgrounds grant their ancestral language free, separate from the INT-based count.

Talents & Expertise

Talents grant Advantage on relevant checks — the universal expertise system every archetype has access to, tied to Background rather than class. See Backgrounds for the full list, and Archetypes for every archetype's complete kit — Grit Maneuvers, Skills, Tradition Options, and Dragonmarks.
